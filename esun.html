PRODUCT DESIGN CASE STUDY

Complex stock-screening rules.
Simpler strategy building.

E.SUN Securities · E-Trader strategy stock screening

ROLE

Product Designer

SCOPE

Strategy creation & usability

PLATFORM

Mobile app

CONTRIBUTIONS

Research, product requirements, flows,
UI system, testing, and design QA

E-Trader’s strategy stock-screening feature lets investors combine financial indicators, technical signals, and ownership data to find stocks that match their criteria. The design challenge was to make those detailed settings easier to find, understand, and change.

I designed the strategy-building flow and interface, then compared two interaction models across six core tasks. The work also established a foundation for future AI assistance: recommendations based on the user’s configured criteria, with those settings available to review and adjust.

CHALLENGE

Investors needed detailed screening rules, but finding the right conditions and editing their parameters added friction to strategy building.

STRATEGY

Organise conditions into clear categories, make parameters readable, and keep selected rules accessible throughout the flow.

RESULTS

Version 2.0 recorded a higher usability score and fewer assisted completions for condition selection. Watchlist actions remained a priority for improvement.

01 / PROBLEM

Different investors. Detailed rules.
A foundation for future AI assistance.

The stock screener needed to support investors with different levels of experience and different approaches to selecting stocks. First-time users needed help understanding where to start, while experienced users needed access to detailed conditions and precise settings.

The challenge was to make that depth manageable today while preparing for future AI assistance. Recommendations would be guided by the user’s configured criteria, making clear, editable settings an essential part of the experience.

01 / INVESTOR NEEDS

Support different types of investors

Investors bring different screening experience and preferences. The flow needed to offer a clear starting point for newcomers while preserving the flexibility experienced users need to build their own strategies.

02 / CONFIGURE

Make detailed parameters easier to understand

Timeframes, thresholds, and comparison rules needed to be readable and straightforward to edit.

03 / FUTURE AI ASSISTANCE

Build a foundation for AI assistance

Future AI recommendations would use the investor’s configured conditions. The interface needed to make those inputs clear and adjustable, establishing a foundation for assistance that reduces the effort of finding matching stocks.

02 / STRATEGY

Make complex strategies
easier to build.

I organised strategy building around a familiar sequence: browse indicator categories, select conditions, and review the settings. This gives first-time users a manageable starting point while preserving detailed controls for experienced investors.

01 / ORGANISE

Group indicators into clear categories

I grouped screening indicators into four categories: Ownership, Technical, Financial, and Sectors. Breaking complex information into recognisable groups helps users narrow their search and find conditions relevant to their investment approach.

02 / SELECT

Make selection simple, with room for detail

Inspired by an ordering flow, users can browse conditions, add them to a strategy, and adjust their settings. The initial selection stays straightforward, while detailed timeframes and thresholds give experienced investors more control over their criteria.

03 / MAKE VISIBLE

Make user-defined criteria visible for AI assistance

Selected conditions and parameters remain visible and editable, so users can understand the criteria guiding future AI recommendations. This gives them a way to review and adjust those inputs, supporting trust in the assisted experience.

03 / VALIDATION

Two interaction models.
The same six tasks.

I used a comparative usability study to examine how easily users could operate the two prototypes. Report 4.0 records eight task responses per version, from colleagues outside the digital product department with and without stock-screener experience.

The six tasks covered creating a strategy, selecting conditions, adjusting parameters, removing a condition, adding a stock to a watchlist, and renaming a strategy. Think-aloud observations and interviews helped explain the task results alongside ASQ and SUS scores.

04 / OUTCOME

A clearer strategy-building flow.
A foundation for future assistance.

The internal study supported version 2.0 as the stronger basis for the next iteration, while identifying specific actions that still needed work. The immediate priorities were clearer entry points, easier movement between conditions, and more recognisable editing and watchlist controls.

The longer-term direction is AI-assisted stock selection based on the user’s configured rules. Establishing a usable settings flow prepares the product for that extension. The results shown here validate prototype usability; AI recommendation quality and the usability of the future assisted journey still require evaluation.
